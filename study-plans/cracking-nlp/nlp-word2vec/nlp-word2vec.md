# <span style="font-size: 20px;">Word2Vec (Skip-gram with Negative Sampling)</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Word2Vec is a family of models that learn dense, low-dimensional word embeddings by training a shallow neural network on word co-occurrence patterns. The Skip-gram variant predicts context words given a center word, and Negative Sampling makes training tractable by replacing the expensive softmax with a binary classification task.</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Skip-gram Model</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Given a center word $w_c$, the Skip-gram model tries to predict each context word $w_o$ within a fixed window. The model maintains two embedding matrices:</span>

* <span style="font-size: 14px;">$W_{in}$ (input/center embeddings): each row is the embedding for a word when it appears as the center</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">$W_{out}$ (output/context embeddings): each row is the embedding for a word when it appears as context</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">The probability of observing context word $o$ given center word $c$ under the full softmax is:</span>

$$P(w_o | w_c) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{u}_o \cdot \mathbf{v}_c)}{\sum_{k=1}^{V} \exp(\mathbf{u}_k \cdot \mathbf{v}_c)}$$

<span style="font-size: 14px;">where $\mathbf{v}_c$ is the center embedding and $\mathbf{u}_o$ is the context embedding. Computing this softmax is $O(V)$ per training example, which is impractical for large vocabularies.</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Negative Sampling</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Negative Sampling replaces the softmax with a binary classification objective. For each positive (center, context) pair, we sample $k$ negative context words and train the model to distinguish real context from noise.</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">The objective for a single positive pair $(c, o)$ with negatives $\{n_1, \ldots, n_k\}$:</span>

$$\mathcal{L} = -\log \sigma(\mathbf{u}_o \cdot \mathbf{v}_c) - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \log \sigma(-\mathbf{u}_{n_i} \cdot \mathbf{v}_c)$$

<span style="font-size: 14px;">where $\sigma$ is the sigmoid function. The first term pushes positive pairs together; the sum pushes negative pairs apart.</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Negative Sampling Distribution</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Negative words are sampled from a modified unigram distribution:</span>

$$P(w) = \frac{f(w)^{3/4}}{\sum_{w'} f(w')^{3/4}}$$

<span style="font-size: 14px;">where $f(w)$ is the frequency count of word $w$. The $3/4$ exponent smooths the distribution - rare words get sampled more often than their raw frequency would suggest, and frequent words get sampled less.</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Gradient Updates</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">For a (center, context) pair with SGD, the gradients are:</span>

* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Positive context**: $\Delta \mathbf{u}_o = (\sigma(\mathbf{u}_o \cdot \mathbf{v}_c) - 1) \cdot \mathbf{v}_c$</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Negative context**: $\Delta \mathbf{u}_n = \sigma(\mathbf{u}_n \cdot \mathbf{v}_c) \cdot \mathbf{v}_c$</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Center word**: $\Delta \mathbf{v}_c = (\sigma(\mathbf{u}_o \cdot \mathbf{v}_c) - 1) \cdot \mathbf{u}_o + \sum_{i} \sigma(\mathbf{u}_{n_i} \cdot \mathbf{v}_c) \cdot \mathbf{u}_{n_i}$</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Context and negative vectors are updated immediately. The center gradient is accumulated across the positive and all negative samples, then applied once.</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Training Pairs</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">Given a corpus flattened into a word sequence, skip-gram pairs are generated by sliding a window of size $w$ around each word. For a center word at position $i$, context words are at positions $[i-w, i+w]$ excluding $i$ itself, clamped to valid indices.</span>

<span style="font-size: 14px;">For example, with window=1 and sequence ["a", "b", "c"], the pairs are: (a,b), (b,a), (b,c), (c,b).</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Properties of Learned Embeddings</span>

* <span style="font-size: 14px;">Words in similar contexts get similar embeddings (distributional hypothesis)</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">Linear algebraic relationships emerge: $\mathbf{v}_{king} - \mathbf{v}_{man} + \mathbf{v}_{woman} \approx \mathbf{v}_{queen}$</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">Cosine similarity between embeddings correlates with semantic similarity</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">Only the input embeddings $W_{in}$ are typically used as the final word vectors</span>

---

## <span style="font-size: 16px;">Common Interview Follow-ups</span>

* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Why negative sampling over full softmax?** Full softmax requires normalizing over the entire vocabulary ($O(V)$ per example). Negative sampling reduces this to $O(k)$ where $k$ is the number of negatives, typically 5-20</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Why the 3/4 exponent?** It smooths the sampling distribution so rare words are not starved of gradient signal while common words do not dominate training</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**CBOW vs Skip-gram?** CBOW predicts the center word from context (faster, better for frequent words). Skip-gram predicts context from center (slower, better for rare words and small datasets)</span>
* <span style="font-size: 14px;">**Word2Vec and matrix factorization?** Levy and Goldberg (2014) showed Skip-gram with negative sampling implicitly factorizes a shifted PMI (Pointwise Mutual Information) matrix</span>
